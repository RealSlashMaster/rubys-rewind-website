Bacon-Wrapped Water Chestnuts

A real family recipe · Bake 25–30 minutes at 400°F

These were everywhere in the seventies, and Ruby has made them every Christmas for "probably a hundred years." Some people mix ketchup into the soy sauce. Ruby doesn't mess with tradition.

Ingredients

  • 1 can whole water chestnuts
  • ¼ cup soy sauce (optional)
  • 4 slices bacon, not thick-cut; cut each slice in half, then in half lengthwise
  • ¼ cup packed brown sugar, give or take
  • Toothpicks
  • Cooking spray or parchment paper

Instructions

  1. If using soy sauce, marinate the water chestnuts in it for about half an hour.
  2. Wrap each chestnut in a piece of bacon, just enough to go around and overlap a little. Avoid layers of bacon; it needs to get crispy.
  3. Secure the overlap with a toothpick.
  4. Press brown sugar into the bacon. Be generous. You can never have too much.
  5. Arrange on a rack over a baking sheet. Spray the rack with cooking spray, or better yet, use parchment.
  6. Bake at 400°F for about 25–30 minutes, until the bacon is crispy but not burnt. Start keeping an eye on them after 20 minutes.
Ruby TipBuy WHOLE water chestnuts. Ask her about the Christmas Eve she opened two cans of sliced ones.
Party Food

Southwest Dip in a Bread Bowl

Bake 1 to 1½ hours at 350°F

Mix it, fill it, wrap it, bake it. That's it. Serve it with the torn bread, veggies and chips, and watch it disappear.

Ingredients

  • 1 block cream cheese, softened
  • 1 cup sour cream
  • 1 cup salsa
  • 3 cups shredded cheddar
  • 1 round pumpernickel loaf (about 2 lb)
  • Veggies and chips, for serving

Instructions

  1. Slice the top off the loaf about 2 inches down and save it as the lid.
  2. Hollow the loaf by tearing out the center, leaving about a 1-inch wall. Save the torn bread for dipping.
  3. Mix the cream cheese, sour cream, salsa and cheddar until thick, creamy and well blended.
  4. Fill the bread bowl, put the lid back on and wrap the whole loaf in heavy-duty foil.
  5. Bake at 350°F for 1 to 1½ hours.
  6. Unwrap, move to a serving board, take off the lid and serve hot with the torn bread, veggies and chips.
Ruby TipDon't toss the bread you tore out. That's the best dipper on the board.

Peppered Bacon BLT on Skillet-Toasted Sourdough

Marinated tomatoes · dressed greens · crisp peppered bacon

Ingredients

  • 2 thick slices sourdough bread
  • 2 to 3 tbsp mayonnaise, divided
  • 4 to 6 slices peppered bacon, cooked until crisp
  • 1 large ripe tomato, sliced
  • 1 handful leaf lettuce or mixed greens
  • 1 to 2 tsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 to 2 tsp red wine vinegar or white balsamic vinegar
  • Kosher salt and freshly cracked black pepper, to taste

Instructions

  1. Prep the tomatoes and greens. Lay the tomato slices on a plate, drizzle lightly with olive oil and a splash of vinegar, season with salt and pepper and set aside.
  2. Toast the bread. Spread mayonnaise on one side of each slice and toast mayo-side down in a skillet over medium heat until golden and crispy.
  3. Assemble. Spread a little more mayo on the crispy inside of the bread. Layer the tomatoes, bacon and greens on the bottom slice.
  4. Finish and serve. Toss the greens with a tiny extra drop of oil and vinegar if you like, close the sandwich, slice and serve right away.
Ruby TipToasting the bread in mayonnaise instead of butter gives you a rich, golden crust and extra flavor in every bite.
Soups

Wedding Soup

Chicken broth, homemade meatballs, spinach, pasta and Romano croutons.

Ingredients

  • Chicken broth
  • 1 roaster chicken (largest you can find)
  • 1 tbsp salt
  • 1½ tsp parsley
  • 1 whole onion (quartered)
  • Celery (6-8 stalks, rough chop)
  • 6 quarts water
  • Chicken soup base (Better Than Bouillon) 4 tbsp
  • Meatballs
  • 1 lb ground beef
  • 1 cup seasoned bread crumbs
  • ¼ cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1 tsp parsley, finely chopped
  • ¼ tsp garlic powder
  • ½ cup milk
  • 2 eggs, well beaten
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Homemade croutons
  • 1 cup bread crumbs
  • 1 cup grated Romano cheese
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 12 eggs, well beaten
  • 1 tsp chopped parsley
  • To finish
  • 1 2 ounces fresh chopped spinach
  • 1¼ cups acini de pepe
  • Grated Romano cheese to serve

Instructions

  1. Cook the chicken: Put chicken, salt, parsley, celery, and onion into 6 quarts of water. Cook 3 hours or until fall off the bone tender.
  2. Finish the broth: Remove the chicken from the broth, debone and shred. Strain the broth, removing the onion and celery. Return the shredded meat to the broth. Stir in chicken soup base.
  3. Make the meatballs: Combine ground beef, bread crumbs, Parmesan, parsley, garlic powder, milk, beaten eggs, salt, and pepper. Form small meatballs.
  4. Bake the meatballs: Place on a baking sheet. Bake at 350°F for about 15 minutes. Drain on paper towels.
  5. Mix the croutons: Combine bread crumbs, Romano cheese, baking powder, beaten eggs, and chopped parsley.
  6. Bake until golden: Pour onto a buttered, parchment-lined baking sheet. Bake at 350°F until brown, about 20 minutes. Cut into cubes while hot.
  7. Finish the soup: Add the chopped spinach, meatballs and acini de pepe to the soup. Mix well and heat through.
  8. Serve: Serve piping hot with croutons and grated Romano cheese.

Kahlua Caramel Tarts

Makes about 4 dozen mini tarts · Bake 15–25 minutes at 350°F

Cream cheese crust, melted caramel with a splash of Kahlua, and a fluffy frosting on top. One bite and they're gone, so make the whole batch.

Ingredients

  • Crust:
  • 6 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 2 sticks margarine (or butter)
  • 2 cups flour
  • Filling:
  • 1 bag (11–14 oz) caramels, unwrapped
  • 3 tbsp Kahlua or rum
  • ½ cup evaporated milk
  • Frosting:
  • ¼ cup Crisco
  • 1 cup sugar
  • ½ tsp vanilla
  • ½ cup margarine, softened
  • ⅓ cup evaporated milk

Instructions

  1. Make the crust: Mix the cream cheese, margarine and flour like a pie crust. Press into tart pans and prick each tart with a fork.
  2. Bake the shells: Bake at 350°F for 15–25 minutes, until light brown. Let them cool.
  3. Make the filling: Melt the caramels, Kahlua and evaporated milk together in a double boiler, stirring until smooth. Fill the tart shells.
  4. Make the frosting: Beat the Crisco, sugar, vanilla, margarine and evaporated milk until fluffy.
  5. Finish: Frost the cooled tarts.
Ruby TipThe note in the margin says it all: this makes too much filling, even with an 11 oz bag of caramels. Pick up the smaller bag.
